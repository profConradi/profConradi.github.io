Simone Conradi holds a PhD in theoretical physics, about lattice QCD from the University of Genoa (2008). After a decade in industry, he started consulting on mathematical models and machine learning. Now he teaches computer science at ITIS Delpozzo in Cuneo, he develops innovative teaching methodologies integrating Python coding into scientific disciplines, particularly mathematics and physics, and explores the pedagogical applications of artificial intelligence in education. He authored *Intelligenza artificiale Cogito ergo sum?* (Zanichelli, 2022).

### The art of mathematical discovery

As a visual artist, Simone reveals mathematics' hidden beauty using Python. He creates minimal code that allows mathematical patterns to emerge naturally, viewing his work not as illustrations of equations but as revelations of elegant complexity.

The process is direct: mathematical idea → Python code → plotting. These images emerge from precise mathematical processes—polynomial equations with complex parameters, root distributions in the complex plane, algorithmic explorations of mathematical structures. The mesmerizing patterns naturally emerge, representing how mathematical objects behave as their parameters vary.

### Computational approach

Using custom Python code with Matplotlib as the primary graphical library, these visualizations transform mathematical structures into visually striking images. This computational approach reveals hidden mathematical relationships that would otherwise remain abstract within the equations themselves.

Each work documents an unexplored mathematical territory, where computational rigor and aesthetic sensibility converge to celebrate mathematics' intrinsic elegance—beauty not programmed but *discovered*, emerging naturally from the underlying algebraic structure.
